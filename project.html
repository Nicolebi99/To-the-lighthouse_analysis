1.1 Sample

The sample includes 14 participants aged 24-30, all with master’s degrees and from the Veneto region. We divided the group into humanists, i.e., those who studied in classical fields (such as literature and law), and non-humanists, from scientific-economic fields (economics, engineering). In addition, we included a comparison with the segmentation performed by GPT-4 (ChatGPT), to analyze whether and how an artificial intelligence model differed from human judgments in the perception of narrative events; and the BOOK, i.e., the original paragraph of the first chapter of to the lighthouse in books. - H : Humanist (20, 19, 21, 30, 19, 54) - NH : Non-humanist (86, 79, 69, 57, 77, 68, 49, 87) - B: Book : 28 (division of paragraphs in the original text) - GPT-4: 63.

1.2 Materials

During the analysis phase of the segmentations from the first chapter of To the Lighthouse, we used Excel to create a dataset containing binary information about the segmentations identified by participants. This dataset was then processed using various Python libraries to create visualizations. Python libraries used: pandas, matplotlib, numpy, seaborn.

1.3 Analysis

Here we present the results of the segmentation process, highlighting key patterns and insights discovered during the analysis.

1.4 Results

In the first section of the study, the method of Text Segmentation, a fundamental tool for analyzing the division of text into events, was introduced in both human and automated methods. To this end, it was decided to compare the first chapter of the novel To the Lighthouse with segmentations obtained from a human sample (53 segmentations) and from GPT-4 (63 segmentations). To measure the similarity between segmentations, Hamming and Jaccard distances were calculated by transforming the data into binary vectors (where 1 indicates the presence of a segmentation in a sentence, and 0 its absence, out of a total of 341 sentences). The results showed a Hamming distance of 0.138 (similarity: 86.16%) and a Jaccard distance of 0.564 (similarity: 43.60%). The higher similarity shown by Hamming’s distance reflects the many common points where segmentation does not occur, while Jaccard’s distance focuses exclusively on segmentation points, showing that less than half of the segmentations coincide. The human sample was then separated into two groups: Humanists and Non Humanists, based on their undergraduate background, on which the same distance analysis was carried out, comparing in the former case, the textual segmentation provided by the editorial paragraphing, then the division of sections within the book; and that by GPT-4 containing the 63 segments. Both in the individual cases and in the overall analysis, humanists showed a greater closeness to editorial paragraphing, with a mean Hamming distance of 0.08 (similarity: 91.98%) and a Jaccard distance of 0.645 (similarity: 35.55%). In detail, the minimum values found for humanists were a Hamming distance of 0.137 (86.3%) and a Jaccard distance of 0.734 (26.6%), while the maximum values were 0.035 (96.5%) and 0.387 (61.3%), respectively. For Non Humanists, Hamming’s average distance was 0.153 (84.73%) and Jaccard’s was 0.686 (31.36%). The lowest values recorded were a Hamming distance of 0.202 (79.8%) and a Jaccard distance of 0.758 (24.2%), while the highest values were 0.111 (88.9%) and 0.629 (37.1%), respectively. Analyzing the GPT-4 data in relation to the two groups, it is observed that humanists obtained a mean Hamming distance of 0.145 (similarity: 85.43%) and a Jaccard distance of 0.714 (similarity: 28.58%). Non Humanists, on the other hand, reported a mean Hamming distance of 0.132 (similarity: 86.71%) and a Jaccard distance of 0.451 (similarity: 54.86%), indicating a greater affinity with this group than humanists in the segmentation generated by GPT-4. These data suggest that humanists tend to segment text more like editorial paragraphes, with fewer segmentations, probably related to a more literary approach. In contrast, non-humanists exhibit more frequent and standardized segmentation, reflecting a more analytical approach. GPT-4 stands as an average between the two groups, with a numerical distribution of segmentations similar to humanists and greater agreement with Non Humanists at segmentation points.
